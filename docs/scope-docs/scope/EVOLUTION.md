# Scope's Evolution

Version: 1.0

A roadmap for *personality*, not features. Every entry below describes a
kind of deepening in who Scope is understood to be, not a thing to build.
The actual moments that realize each year's theme are designed one at a
time, against `DESIGN_FILTER.md`, and tracked in `LIVING_SCOPE.md` as they
ship — this document is about what *kind* of change belongs at each
distance from launch, and, just as importantly, what kind never belongs
at any distance.

---

## Year 1 — establishing rhythm

The work of Year 1 is proving that Scope has a life that continues
whether or not he's watched, at timescales a single sitting can't show on
its own: a day boundary, an absence and a return, true stillness. Nothing
shipped this year should require the visitor to have any history with the
site yet beyond what a single visit can establish — the goal is a
foundation that later years' longer-arc moments can be trusted to build
on, not a personality trait in itself.

This is `LIVING_SCOPE.md`'s Tier 1 and most of Tier 2: time-of-day drift,
first-visit-of-the-day, returning after absence, stillness, the seasonal
tint, golden-hour light, the patient settle. By the end of Year 1, the
site should feel like it has *rhythm* — daily and occasional variation —
without yet feeling like it remembers anyone specifically.

## Year 2 — establishing memory

Year 2 is where Scope is allowed to know things about a *specific*
returning visitor, not just about time passing in general. This is
categorically different from Year 1 and should be treated with
proportionally more care: a personal first-visit anniversary, and the
early, carefully-tuned beginnings of cross-session familiarity
(`LIVING_SCOPE.md` Tier 3's "cross-session familiarity drift" — reserved
for Tier 3 specifically because it needs a year of real return-visit data
before it can be tuned honestly).

The standing rule for anything in this year: memory must always read as
*recognition*, never as *tracking*. "He seems to remember you" is the
target; anything that could be described as "the site remembers your
data" — even if true, even if privacy-respecting, even if only ever
stored client-side — has failed the tone this year is supposed to
establish. If a Year 2 idea can't clear that bar, it waits for Year 3, or
it never ships.

## Year 3 — establishing depth

By Year 3, the moments that ship should be the ones that only a small
fraction of visitors will ever earn or notice — the moon-phase marker, the
rarest tiers of familiarity, whatever new ideas the intervening two years'
own review process has surfaced as worth this level of restraint. This is
the year where "if someone never notices this, that's acceptable" is
taken most literally: Year 3 moments are allowed to be seen by almost
nobody, because by this point the site's own personality is established
enough that most of what makes it feel alive is already load-bearing —
these are the last, quietest layer on top, not the foundation.

## What should never exist, at any year

These are not "not yet" — they are permanent exclusions, regardless of how
much the site has grown or how tempting a specific idea seems in the
moment:

- **A voice, beyond the one existing exception.** `SCOPE.md`/
  `PERSONALITY.md` both carve out exactly one deliberate exception (the
  one-time "Hi. I'm Scope." greeting) and are explicit that it is the
  *only* one, forever. No Living Moment, however rare or earned, is
  license to add a second line of text or a second moment of speech.
- **Arms, or any literal manipulation of objects.** Every physical
  interaction in this project (the Contact letter, any future prop) has
  been deliberately built to move on its own physical logic *around*
  Scope's total lack of limbs — this is not a current limitation waiting
  to be lifted later. It is permanent.
- **A new eye shape, or any shape swap.** `CONSTRAINTS.md`'s "never a
  curve, a star, or any other shape swap" survived a direct, deliberate
  challenge (the crescent-eyes proposal) specifically because the
  alternative — pushing the existing pill further — was judged to
  preserve his identity better than a more literally expressive shape
  would have. That precedent should hold the same way for every future
  proposal to change his silhouette.
- **Gamification, in any form.** No achievements, no counters shown to the
  visitor, no unlockable content, no "you found X" acknowledgment however
  quiet. A Living Moment that starts accumulating state the *visitor* is
  meant to be aware of, rather than state the *system* uses invisibly to
  decide what to show, has crossed into a different genre of product
  entirely.
- **Mascot behaviour.** Anything a brand mascot would do — winking at the
  camera, celebrating a visitor's action back at them, referencing that
  he's "an AI portfolio companion," breaking the fact that he never
  acknowledges being observed — is permanently out of bounds regardless of
  how charming a specific execution might be.
- **Anything whose justification is "it would be a fun feature."** This is
  `DESIGN_FILTER.md`'s closing question, restated as a standing rule
  across every year of this roadmap rather than a per-idea check: fun
  alone has never been, and should never become, sufficient reason for
  Scope to do something.

---

Update this document's Year 1/2/3 sections as real moments ship and real
lessons from building them arrive — but the "never" list above should be
treated as far more durable than the rest of the file. If a future
session is ever tempted to revise an entry off that list, that decision
deserves the same explicit, surfaced-to-the-owner treatment the
crescent-eyes question got, not a quiet edit.
